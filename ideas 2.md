# SeedKeeper.org Design Brainstorm

## Workshop Context
"Cultivating Seeds of Knowledge" — A Native FEWS (Food, Energy, Water, Sovereignty) resource hub for K-12 educators, rooted in Indigenous knowledge systems, Diné cultural pedagogy, and land-based learning. The site must feel alive, grounded, and trustworthy — never sterile or corporate.

## Idea 1: "Desert Modernism" — Southwestern Organic Architecture

**Design Movement:** Inspired by the organic architecture of the American Southwest — think Paolo Soleri's Arcosanti meets contemporary Indigenous design. Clean lines that follow natural forms, like the way adobe walls follow the contour of the land.

**Core Principles:**
1. Horizontal expansiveness — layouts that breathe like the desert horizon
2. Material honesty — textures that feel like sandstone, clay, woven fiber
3. Light as a design element — warm golden tones that shift like desert light
4. Layered depth — content emerges from the landscape, not imposed on it

**Color Philosophy:** A palette drawn directly from the Four Corners landscape. Terracotta (oklch(0.58 0.12 45)) as the primary warmth, sage green (oklch(0.62 0.06 155)) for growth and renewal, deep turquoise (oklch(0.52 0.1 220)) as the sacred water accent, ochre gold (oklch(0.72 0.12 80)) for energy and sun, warm cream (oklch(0.95 0.02 85)) as the canvas. Dark charcoal-brown (oklch(0.25 0.03 60)) for text.

**Layout Paradigm:** "Canyon Scroll" — content flows vertically like descending into a canyon, with wide horizontal sections that span edge-to-edge, alternating with narrower focused content corridors. Category pages use a staggered masonry layout reminiscent of cliff dwellings.

**Signature Elements:**
1. Geometric border patterns inspired by Navajo textile motifs — used as section dividers and card accents (thin, respectful, never dominant)
2. Organic wave dividers between sections that echo mesa silhouettes and desert landforms
3. Subtle grain texture overlay on backgrounds that feels like sandstone

**Interaction Philosophy:** Interactions should feel like discovery — cards gently rise on hover like seeds emerging from soil, filters slide in like opening a woven basket, transitions are smooth and unhurried like water finding its path.

**Animation:** Scroll-triggered fade-ups with slight parallax on hero imagery. Cards scale gently (1.02) on hover with a warm shadow bloom. Page transitions use a horizontal wipe. Loading states use a growing seed motif.

**Typography System:** Display: "Playfair Display" (serif) for headings — grounded authority. Body: "Source Sans 3" (sans-serif) — clean, highly readable, educator-friendly. Monospace accents for metadata tags.

## Idea 2: "Woven Knowledge" — Textile-Inspired Information Design

**Design Movement:** Drawing from the visual language of Navajo weaving and textile arts — where every thread has purpose and every pattern tells a story. Information architecture mirrors the structure of a loom: warp (categories) and weft (resources) creating a unified fabric.

**Core Principles:**
1. Interlocking structure — elements connect visually like threads in a weave
2. Pattern as meaning — geometric patterns carry navigational purpose
3. Warm materiality — the site should feel handmade, not machine-generated
4. Rhythmic repetition — consistent visual rhythm creates comfort and trust

**Color Philosophy:** Drawn from natural dye palettes. Deep indigo (oklch(0.32 0.08 265)) as the grounding dark, natural cream/undyed wool (oklch(0.94 0.02 90)) as the primary background, brick red (oklch(0.52 0.14 30)) for action and emphasis, sage (oklch(0.65 0.05 150)) for secondary elements, dusty gold (oklch(0.70 0.10 80)) for highlights. The palette should feel like it could exist in a weaving.

**Layout Paradigm:** "Loom Grid" — a structured grid system where content cards interlock like threads. The main resource library uses a tight, woven grid where cards sit flush with minimal gaps, creating a tapestry of knowledge. Sidebar navigation uses vertical threading.

**Signature Elements:**
1. Thin geometric line patterns (diamonds, zigzags) as borders and dividers — referencing textile patterns
2. Cards with a subtle woven texture background
3. A "thread" visual connecting related resources — literally drawing lines between cards

**Interaction Philosophy:** Pulling a thread — hovering on a resource highlights its connections. Filtering feels like selecting threads on a loom. The experience of browsing should feel like running your hand across woven fabric.

**Animation:** Cards weave into view on scroll — alternating from left and right. Filter transitions use a vertical slide like threads being pulled. Hover states show a subtle weave pattern emerging on card borders.

**Typography System:** Display: "Bitter" (serif slab) — sturdy, grounded, like a loom frame. Body: "Nunito Sans" — warm, rounded, approachable for educators. Small caps for category labels.

## Idea 3: "Living Earth" — Regenerative Design System

**Design Movement:** Biophilic design meets editorial clarity. Inspired by the regenerative agriculture movement itself — the design should feel like it's growing, alive, connected to cycles. Think of a beautifully designed field guide crossed with a modern editorial magazine.

**Core Principles:**
1. Cyclical navigation — content organized by seasons, cycles, and relationships
2. Rooted typography — text feels planted on the page, not floating
3. Breathing layouts — generous whitespace that feels like open land
4. Layered earth tones — depth created through overlapping warm neutrals

**Color Philosophy:** Earth strata as palette. Deep soil brown (oklch(0.30 0.04 55)) as the anchor, warm sand (oklch(0.90 0.03 80)) as primary background, terracotta clay (oklch(0.55 0.13 40)) for primary actions, deep water turquoise (oklch(0.48 0.09 225)) for the water/knowledge accent, fresh sage (oklch(0.68 0.08 148)) for growth indicators, golden sun (oklch(0.78 0.13 85)) for energy highlights. Each category gets its own earth-tone accent.

**Layout Paradigm:** "Field Guide" — asymmetric editorial layouts with a strong left margin for navigation and annotations. Content pages use a wide main column with a narrow sidebar for metadata, seasonal notes, and related resources. The homepage uses full-bleed imagery with overlaid text blocks positioned like field notes.

**Signature Elements:**
1. Seasonal indicator badges — small circular icons showing planting/harvest/dormant seasons, connected to the Diné calendar
2. Hand-drawn botanical illustration accents (subtle, in margins and empty states)
3. Earth-tone gradient backgrounds that shift like soil layers

**Interaction Philosophy:** Growth-based — elements emerge upward like plants. Hover states "bloom" with additional information. The search experience should feel like digging — going deeper reveals more. Seasonal filters rotate like a calendar wheel.

**Animation:** Upward growth animations on scroll (elements rise from bottom with slight spring physics). Category tiles have a gentle breathing scale animation on idle. Page transitions use a vertical reveal like turning soil. Loading uses a seed-to-sprout micro-animation.

**Typography System:** Display: "DM Serif Display" — elegant, editorial authority with organic curves. Body: "DM Sans" — clean, modern, pairs perfectly with the serif. The contrast between serif headings and sans body creates the field-guide editorial feel.

## Selected Approach: Idea 3 — "Living Earth" Regenerative Design System

This approach best serves the seedkeeper.org mission because:
- The field guide editorial layout naturally supports a resource library with rich metadata
- The seasonal indicator system directly maps to the cultural teaching considerations in the resources
- The biophilic, growth-oriented interaction philosophy perfectly embodies "cultivating seeds of knowledge"
- The earth-strata color palette honors the land-based pedagogy central to the curriculum
- The asymmetric editorial layout avoids the generic centered-grid trap while remaining highly functional
